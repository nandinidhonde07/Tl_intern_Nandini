Week 4 – Day 4 Documentation

Solar Dryer Manufacturing and Assembly Process

Step 1: Fixing the Laser-Cut Polycarbonate Sheet

The laser-cut polycarbonate sheet was aligned with the solar dryer frame. Screw positions were marked carefully according to the design dimensions.

A drilling machine was used to create holes at the marked locations. Screws and washers were inserted to secure the sheet firmly onto the frame.

The transparent polycarbonate sheet acts as a protective cover while maintaining visibility inside the solar dryer and providing structural rigidity.

Step 2: Marking GI Sheets for Trays

Based on the previously completed 3D tray designs, dimensions were accurately transferred onto galvanized iron (GI) sheets.

Each tray outline was marked carefully to ensure proper cutting, folding, and final assembly.

A total of eight tray blanks were prepared for fabrication.

Step 3: Cutting the GI Sheets

The marked tray outlines were cut using sheet metal cutting tools.

After cutting, all edges were smoothed and cleaned to remove sharp burrs.

The completed tray blanks were stacked together and inspected to verify dimensional consistency and uniformity.

Step 4: Folding with Local Tray Maker

The prepared GI sheet blanks were taken to a local tray fabrication workshop.

Using industrial bending equipment, the tray side walls were folded upward according to the SolidWorks design dimensions.

Corners were reinforced and aligned properly to maintain structural strength and improve durability.

Each tray was shaped precisely to match the 3D CAD model used during design.

Step 5: Final Tray Assembly

All eight fabricated trays were inspected for dimensional accuracy.

Sharp edges were deburred to ensure safe handling and operation.

The trays were stacked and aligned within the solar dryer structure to verify proper fitting and spacing.

Successful alignment confirmed readiness for final integration into the solar dryer assembly.

✨ Outcome

  • The polycarbonate sheet was successfully fixed using screws and washers.
  • The transparent enclosure structure was completed.
  • Eight GI trays were fabricated, folded, and inspected.
  • All trays matched the design dimensions and assembly requirements.
  • The trays were prepared for installation inside the solar dryer.
  • This stage marked the transition from CAD design to real-world manufacturing and assembly.
